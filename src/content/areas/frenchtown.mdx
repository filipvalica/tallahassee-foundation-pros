## Foundation repair in Frenchtown

Frenchtown is one of Tallahassee's oldest and most historically significant neighborhoods, a working-class community near downtown with a housing stock that includes some of the oldest residential construction in the city. Many homes here were built in the 1940s and 1950s on block or poured-concrete foundations that haven't been evaluated since original construction.

That matters because 70-year-old foundations in Frenchtown have been through decades of clay shrink-swell cycles with no modern drainage mitigation. Lots in this neighborhood are small, grade is frequently negative toward the structure, and deferred maintenance on older homes means foundation warning signs often go unaddressed until they become urgent.

Landlords with rental properties in Frenchtown represent a significant portion of our calls from this area. Tenants notice sticking doors, sloped floors, and visible cracks, and landlords who haven't inspected the foundation in years are often dealing with multiple issues at once.

## What we see in Frenchtown

- **Block foundation movement**: older block foundations without modern waterproofing absorb moisture cycling and develop horizontal cracks; some show inward bowing that requires carbon fiber stabilization
- **Slab settlement from poor drainage**: negative grade around foundation perimeters is common in Frenchtown; each rain season deposits water directly against older foundation walls
- **Active cracks reopening after prior patching**: cosmetic repairs done on moving foundations are the most common repair history we see; structural correction is what stops the pattern
- **Step cracks in exterior brick**: stair-step cracking on brick chimneys and exterior walls is consistent with differential settlement and often indicates one corner or section has moved more than the rest
