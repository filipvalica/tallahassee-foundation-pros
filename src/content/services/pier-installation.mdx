## Pier installation in Tallahassee

When a Tallahassee slab settles, there are two problems to solve: stopping the movement and restoring the grade. Piers address both, they transfer the foundation load past the unstable soil to a stable bearing layer, then allow hydraulic lifting to restore the original elevation.

Tallahassee's soil is not uniform. Sandy surface layers give way to red clay bands, and beneath that is limestone karst. That karst layer is what makes Tallahassee soil interesting from an engineering standpoint, helical piers reach the limestone and anchor into it. Steel push piers drive past the sandy/clay transition and find the same stable layer. The right choice depends on where the stable strata sits under your specific lot.

## Common situations that require piers

- A corner of the house has settled 1–3 inches relative to the rest of the slab
- Doors in one area of the home have all started sticking at the same time
- The foundation crack is active, growing rather than stable
- A previous crack repair has re-opened (indicates the root movement was never addressed)
- Pre-purchase inspection found documented settlement that a cosmetic fix won't resolve

## What the installation process looks like

**Step 1: Assessment.** We review the inspection findings and specify pier type, count, and placement in writing. You approve before we start.

**Step 2: Excavation.** We excavate small access points at each pier location along the foundation perimeter. Typical excavation is 24"×24" and 18"–24" deep.

**Step 3: Installation.** Each pier is driven or screwed to the load-bearing stratum. We monitor load readings throughout to confirm we've reached refusal (stable bearing) on each pier.

**Step 4: Lifting.** Once all piers are installed, we use synchronized hydraulic jacks to raise the settled section. We lift incrementally and check interior elements (doors, windows, floors) as we go.

**Step 5: Backfill and cleanup.** We backfill, compact, and clean the work area. The excavation sites are packed to match the surrounding grade.
