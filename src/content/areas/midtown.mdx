## Foundation repair in Midtown Tallahassee

Midtown is one of Tallahassee's oldest residential neighborhoods, a mix of Victorian-era homes from the late 1800s, Craftsman bungalows from the 1920s, and mid-century construction from the postwar boom. The housing stock near FSU's campus is diverse, but the soil under all of it is the same: sandy clay over limestone karst that shifts with every wet-dry cycle.

Older homes in Midtown carry accumulated risk. A house built in 1940 has been through 85 years of clay expansion and shrinkage. Foundation walls that were poured without modern waterproofing have absorbed decades of moisture cycles. Slab cracks that started hairline-thin have widened with each season.

The karst factor is relevant for Midtown specifically. While catastrophic sinkholes are rare, minor dissolution of the limestone beneath the sandy layers creates subtle voids that can cause localized settlement, a corner drops, a crack opens, and the homeowner assumes it's age. It may be, but it may also be a micro-void that's worth investigating.

## Midtown's foundation patterns

- **Slab cracking from clay shrinkage**: fall dry periods are when Midtown homeowners most often notice new cracks at door frames and interior corners
- **Settlement near the foundation perimeter**: older homes with negative grade around the foundation compound clay-swell damage every rainy season
- **Sticking doors and windows**: the most consistent symptom in pre-1960 construction; when framing shifts, anything fitted to that frame binds
- **Bowing stem walls on older block foundations**: Midtown has a higher concentration of older block construction than newer Tallahassee neighborhoods
